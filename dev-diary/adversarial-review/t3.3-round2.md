### Silent intervals stay with the bed

The synthetic bed is an 80 Hz stereo sine at volume 0.2. Speech is a 2 kHz tone from 1 s to 2 s.

Seconds 0.2 through 0.8 of bed and mix both measure -38.2 dB mean and -35.2 dB maximum.
Those decoded windows share SHA-1 `05d648f957717e01fd4316bd70af29385cf2ba8a`.
Lowpass RMS is -38.174012 dB on both. The delta is 0.000000 dB.

Seconds 2.5 through 3.5 repeat that exact match after speech ends.
The 2.05 s to 2.25 s release window also matches the bed SHA-1.

A constant `volume=0.20` plus default `amix` drops the same silent lowpass window to -58.3 dB.
That is 20.1 dB below the bed. Duck does not apply that cut.

### Bed dips under speech

Lowpass at 200 Hz isolates the bed.
The 1.2 s to 1.8 s bed window measures -38.2 dB mean.
The same window in the mix measures -43.5 dB mean. That is a 5.3 dB dip.
Lowpass RMS moves from -38.174013 dB to -43.484869 dB. That is -5.310856 dB.

Decoded `mix - speech` versus the bed is -5.316684 dB in that window.
Overlay on the same files stays at -38.2 dB. The dip exists only on the Duck path.

### Dubbed speech keeps full volume

Highpass at 800 Hz isolates the voice.
Speech and mix both measure -32.2 dB mean in the 1.2 s to 1.8 s window.
Peaks measure -28.4 dB on both.
Highpass RMS is -32.219776 dB on speech and -32.219732 dB on the mix.
The delta is 0.000044 dB.

The same graph without `normalize=0` measures -38.3 dB mean in that voice band.
That is a 6.1 dB cut. `normalize=0` is present and effective.
