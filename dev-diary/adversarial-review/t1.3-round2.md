## False Positives

I checked four hypotheses and found no defect in three of them.

**The slow single-row insert on Cloud is not a schema defect.** The remediation record
attributes a four minute stall to a synchronous `prior_by_speaker` rebuild under
`deduplicate_merge_projection_mode = 'rebuild'` on SharedMergeTree. I measured instead of
accepting the attribution. Ten single-row inserts into `takes`, which carries the
projection, took 6049, 7000 and 6805 ms across three runs. The identical 10 rows as one
batched insert took 1317, 1222 and 1196 ms. Ten single-row inserts into `commits`, which
carries no projection at all, took 5068, 6065 and 6884 ms. A bare `SELECT 1` from a cold
client cost 959 ms. The projection accounts for roughly 100 ms across 10 rows. The cost is
per-statement round trip, and it is the same with and without a projection. I never
reproduced anything near four minutes. This is a T4.1 client batching matter, and T4.1
already requires batching. The orchestrator should not carry the projection diagnosis into
T4.1.

**ReplacingMergeTree does not contradict the append-only claim for the four original
tables.** `takes`, `commits`, `actions` and `charges` each close their sorting key with a
unique id, so two rows collapse only when they carry the same id. I tried to build a
collision of two genuinely different events on each table and could not without reusing an
id. Two synthesis attempts on one take, two attempts on one segment, and two charges of one
kind on one take all survived as separate rows in the deduped views. `timeline_state` is the
exception, and that is finding 1.

**The three value `charges.kind` enum still breaks nothing.** Round 1 recorded this as a
false positive and it still holds. `internal/cost/cost.go` defines exactly `ChargeSegment`,
`ChargeTranslate` and `ChargeSynthesize`. I re-fired the probe and `kind = 'rewrite'` failed
with `UNKNOWN_ELEMENT_OF_ENUM` on both servers, which is the behaviour the ledger wants.

**The `MergeTree doesn't support FINAL` abort is a partial safety net, not a guard.** I
expected a wrong engine to slip past the preflight guard silently. It does slip past the
guard, but the view creation then refuses `FINAL` over a plain MergeTree and the file exits
non-zero. That accidental catch is real, so I record no separate finding for the missing
engine check. The half-applied database and the misleading message are folded into
finding 4.
