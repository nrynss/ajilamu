## Job Two: The Hunt For A Fourth Way In

The brief told me to assume a fourth path exists until I had tried hard. I found one, and it is
finding 1. I also found a fifth that is smaller, and it is finding 3. Here is everything I
tried, including what failed.

**The exported plan API, attacked through its input type.** This is where it broke. `types.Fit`
is P1 owned and carries a redundant `Delta` beside `Slot` and `Measured`. `PlanStretchWithLimits`
reads `Delta` for the routing and `Measured` for the ratio. So the fourth way in is not a
configuration knob at all. It is a Go composite literal with one field left out. I reached the
2026-09-07 report in one exported call. `internal/ledger` and `internal/fixtures` both already
re-derive the same invariant, which tells me the house convention is derive, never trust, and
that `internal/fit` is the outlier.

**The interaction between the dead band and the ceiling.** Refuted. I swept five budget shapes,
including the ceiling on both sides, a budget of 1e-300 and a budget of 0.001. Each ran across
every integer slot from 1 to 20000 ms, and every measured duration from 0 to three times the
slot. That
is roughly 500 million plans. Zero takes were swallowed by the dead band while exceeding their
own side budget. The algebra holds. The band is `min(40 ms, slot times that side's budget)`, so
anything inside the band is inside the budget by construction. No caller can pick a budget that
hides a rewrite.

**Repeated application.** Real, and finding 4. Six legal steps take segment 8 from a 40.9 percent
underrun to a 9 ms miss. The ceiling binds per call and nothing says so.

**Concurrency.** Real, and finding 3. Both `checkPaths` and `discardOutput` operate by path.

**Slot length, at both extremes and at the boundaries round 1 and round 2 named.** Nothing
broke. I re-derived both boundaries rather than reading them. The first whole millisecond miss
that reaches atempo is 41 ms on a 513 ms slot on the long side. On the short side it is 41 ms on
an 820 ms slot. Both match round 2. Below those the band absorbs every miss the code declines to
stretch, and anything larger routes to a rewrite. I also probed slots of 1 ns, 1 µs, 1 ms, one
hour, one day and 2^62 ns. A 1 ns slot with a 1 ns take plans `none` with a zero band, and a
1 ns slot with a zero length take plans `rewrite`. Nothing panics, nothing overflows, and no
extreme slot reaches atempo through a path a rewrite should own.

**A short take whose corrective ratio sits on a boundary of the reachable interval.** I swept
106,427,127 atempo plans across five budget shapes. The reachable ratio interval is exactly
[0.900000, 1.100000], which confirms remediation round 2's claim. I also found the invariant
behind it. Every reachable atempo plan carries a band of exactly 40 ms, across 87,226,967 plans.
Atempo needs `band < |delta| <= slot times budget`, and the band equals `slot times budget`
whenever that product falls under 40 ms, so a smaller band makes atempo unreachable. That is why
the guard never fires.

**The listening harness and `renderCandidate`.** No escape. `internal/fit` has no
`export_test.go`, no file outside `internal/fit/` declares `package fit`, and `go doc -all
./internal/fit` lists no exported function taking a ratio. The candidate file names are
`seg_N_ratio_R.wav` and `seg_N_original.wav`, so a mistyped `AJILAMU_LISTENING_DIR` cannot
collide with a take name or a fixture name.

**`Fits()` at 0.08 against the split budgets.** Measured, real, and correctly documented. On a
5000 ms slot, fifteen distinct rows at 10 ms steps report `Fits` true, `TooShort` false and
`TooLong` false while `PlanStretch` returns `rewrite`. The package states this in the constant
block, in the `PlanStretchWithLimits` doc, in the handoff, and in a test. The residual risk sits
in `internal/types`, which T2.5 does not own. I judge it under contract notes.
