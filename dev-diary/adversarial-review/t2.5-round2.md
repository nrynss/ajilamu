## `MaxStretchLimit`, Judged

The brief asks me to rule on this specifically. I split the mechanism from the value.

**The mechanism is right, and I could not break it.** A ceiling is the correct shape for this
guard, and the implementation refuses rather than clamps. I attacked it fourteen ways from an
outside package. I tried the zero value struct, a short side set with the long side unset, and the mirror of that. I tried zero, negative, NaN, positive infinity, negative infinity, 0.45, 1.0, and one ULP above 0.15. Every one returns `ErrLimits` from `StretchWithLimits` and `RepairManual` from `PlanStretchWithLimits`. None of them writes a file. One ULP below 0.15 and 0.15
itself are accepted. There is no silent clamping anywhere. I built the clamp explicitly as a
mutation, moving the ceiling into `For` and dropping it from `usableLimit`, and
`TestStretchRejectsUnusableLimits` caught it. I also negated `usableLimit` so NaN would pass,
and the same test caught that. The fail direction is correct throughout. An unresolvable budget
never becomes a wide budget.

**Adding it was right.** Without it a caller resolving 0.45 hands segment 8 back to atempo. The
remediator raised it as a decision rather than a repair, which is the correct disclosure.

**The value is where it falls down**, in two separate ways, which are findings 1 and 2. The
ceiling admits a 15 percent slow down that nobody has heard, while the ladder built to settle
the short side stops at roughly 10 percent. And the suite defends nothing between 0.081 and
0.408, which I proved by shipping a 40.5 percent underrun as a clean repair with a green suite.

**What I would want instead.** Bring the ceiling down to the top of the ladder, or add three rungs to the ladder. Either way a listener can then rule on the ceiling as well as on the default. Pin the chosen number as a literal, never as an expression built from the constant.
