## The Ceiling, Decided

`MaxStretchLimit` is now 0.10, written as a literal in the constant and pinned against a
literal in the test. The brief handed me this decision rather than asking me to re-derive it,
so I implemented it and verified it rather than arguing for it again.

**What 0.10 refuses that 0.15 did not.** Any caller resolved budget between 0.10 and 0.15 on
either side now fails `Valid()` and returns `ErrLimits` from `StretchWithLimits` and
`RepairManual` from `PlanStretchWithLimits`. I checked 0.11, 0.1001, 0.15 and the round 2
mutation value of 0.408, all four report invalid under the new ceiling.

**What it still admits.** `DefaultMaxStretchShort` at 0.05 and `DefaultMaxStretchLong` at 0.08
both sit under 0.10, so the project defaults are unaffected. A caller can still resolve up to
0.10 on either side, which is the listening ladder's lowest rendered rung.

**What it refuses that 0.15 admitted.** Round 2's finding 1 reproduction, `seg_6_try1.wav`
against a 5223 ms slot, ratio 0.8501, a 14.99 percent correction, now returns `ErrUnderrunTooLarge`
under the widest budget the package accepts. Under the old 0.15 ceiling that same call would
have resolved to `atempo` and landed a file on disk.

**Why 0.10 and not some other number.** `dev-diary/PHASE-2-fit-loop.md`'s listening ladder table
renders candidates down to ratio 0.9000 on both segment 1 and segment 6. Nothing softer has
ever been rendered for a listener to judge. A ceiling wider than 0.10 would let a caller resolve
a repair this project has never put in front of an ear. A ceiling narrower than 0.10 would
refuse the ladder's own lowest rung, which makes no sense once the ladder is the instrument the
ceiling answers to.
