## The Dead Band, Judged

I attacked this hardest and I come down on the implementer's side. The constant is sound and the
absolute shape is right.

**Absolute beats relative here.** Both quantities that justify the band are absolute. The
`atempo` residual comes from filter window quantisation and does not scale with take length. The
perceptual line is a fixed number of milliseconds. A relative band would break at both ends. One
percent of the 7040 ms slot is 70 ms, which crosses the perceptual line. One percent of the
1820 ms slot is 18 ms, which sits under the residual I measured on that very take, so the landing
check would reject correct stretches.

**The two justifying numbers hold up.** I measured the residual myself on all eight fixtures at
their corrective ratios. Inside the repair band the largest is 16.61 ms. At segment 8's refused
ratio of 0.5907 it reaches 26.16 ms, which is 25 ms once `media.Duration` rounds. The comment at
lines 43 to 45 says "reached 25 ms and stayed under 20 ms inside the repair band". Both halves
are correct against my numbers. A band of 40 ms leaves 23 ms of headroom over the worst in band
residual.

**Stretching segments 2, 5 and 7 is right, not over correction.** They sit 2.9, 2.5 and 3.4
percent short, which `metrics.json` recorded as `"repair": "none"` and `"outcome": "fits"`.
`project.md` step 5 says the system applies `atempo` within 8 percent in either direction. The
ratios are 0.9708, 0.9752 and 0.9659. A 3 percent slow down is far inside what `atempo` carries
without artefact, and the three takes land 11, 8 and 9 ms from their slots afterwards. Leaving
them alone is what the 2026-09-07 run did, and `observations.md` exists because of it.

**No under correction on long slots.** On the 7110 ms slot the band is 0.56 percent. The band
never grows with the slot, so a long slot gets the tightest relative treatment, not the loosest.

**The boundaries are consistent.** At exactly 40 ms the take is inside the band and is left
alone. At exactly 8 percent the take is repairable and gets `atempo`. Both boundaries put the
take on the gentler side, and `TestPlanStretchHonoursDirectionalThresholds` pins all four edges
at 5000 ms. I mutated `<=` to `<` at line 138 and `>` to `>=` at line 148 and the suite caught
both.

**One correction to the implementer's stated consequence.** The claim is that below a 500 ms slot
no stretch ever happens. The substance is right and the number is not. I searched every integer
slot from 1 to 3000 ms for a delta that plans `atempo`. The first is 513 ms with a 41 ms delta.
Between 500 and 512 ms the band is 40 ms and 8 percent of the slot sits between 40.00 and
40.96 ms, so no whole millisecond fits in the gap. The behaviour is correct either way. Below
that boundary every miss the code declines to stretch is under 41 ms, which is inside the same
tolerance the design accepts at every other slot length. I record this as a correction, not a
finding.

## Seams That Belong To Another Task

The implementer raised four. I judge each one.

**1. The stale short side rows in `testdata/expected/metrics.json`. Real, and it belongs to T2.7
or T0.4.** Rows 2, 5 and 7 carry `"repair": "none"` and `"outcome": "fits"` for takes that sit
2.5 to 3.4 percent short. The Go code plans `atempo` for all three, correctly. T2.7's done when
clause says the Go loop reproduces the test run outputs against fixtures. It will not, on three
of eight segments, and it should not. Nothing breaks today. `internal/fixtures/load.go` reads
only the `takes` duration map from that file and never the `repair` or `outcome` fields, and
`stretch_test.go` declares its own expected repair column. The fixture needs a note saying it
records the Python run's decisions rather than the contract.

**2. `media.Atempo` rounds the ratio it applies. Real and small, and it belongs to T0.3.** At
four decimals the worst case ratio error is 5e-5. On a 7 s take at ratio near 1 that moves the
output by about 0.35 ms, far under any band. My mutation to `%.3f` passed the whole suite, which
shows the precision is unpinned rather than wrong. I would leave `%.4f` alone and note that
nothing tests it.

**3. The threshold split between `MaxStretchShort` and `types.FitThreshold`. Not a defect
today.** See false positive 6. The genuine problem hiding beside it is the direction blind cap
in `deadBand`, which I raise as finding 2 against T2.5 itself.

**4. Take naming for T2.7. Real, and it belongs to T2.7.** The fixture set names one stretched
take per segment, `seg_3_stretched.wav`, with no attempt number. T2.7 must run three attempts
per line and `checkPaths` returns `ErrOutputExists` on the second write to any name. A scheme
like `seg_3_try2_stretched.wav` avoids it. This is T2.7's to choose and `internal/fit` is right
to refuse the overwrite.

**Not raised by the implementer, and worth naming.** `scratch/` is gitignored, so the listening
ladder and its measurements do not survive the commit. Whoever answers the short side question
should paste the verdict into the T2.5 handoff entry, which is finding 6.
